# Narrative bible template (Writer's Room)

Owner: the Narrative Designer. The bible is the **fabula**, the hidden truth in time order. The
player meets it in their own order through the story graph. Worked example:
`examples/case-packs/dead-air-on-the-meridian/NARRATIVE.md`.

```markdown
# NARRATIVE: "<case title>"
> Spoiler wall + pointer to case-file.json

## 0. Logline + the twist (in layers)  — and why it suits the medium
## 1. Setting — place, date, why the circle is closed, the clock, maps/consist tables, room rules
   (fixtures the player can check: counts, locks, windows, plates). Invented names only.
## 2. Cast — per character: public face · secret · tonight (true whereabouts) · their lie(s) ·
   what they give freely · tell signature · herrings attached
## 3. Master timeline — minute by minute, TRUE time base; columns: Time | Event | Trace (which card/testimony proves it)
## 4. True solution — the accusation key; proof categories and the valid cards in each
## 5. Evidence catalogue — ID | item | where/how | what it shows | contradicts | role (essential/supporting/herring/personal)
   + gating rules that keep the solve in the right act
## 6. Lies table — claim ID | who | lie | breaks with | what the crack gives. Killer's locks.
## 7. Branching map — acts, bottlenecks (each with ≥3 routes), proactive clues, storylet unlock table,
   side branches, endings table (condition → ending)
## 8. Red-herring economy — herring | points at | resolution | why it's fair
## 9. Fair-play audit — Knox 1–10 and Van Dine 1–20, line by line
## 10. Solve-length estimate — minimum path (actions + game minutes + real minutes) and typical path
## 11. Adversarial review log — reviewer findings → fixes, and anything deliberately not adopted
## 12. IP distancing — which specific works were checked, what was changed
```

**Rules that bind every case:**
1. **The Three Clue Rule.** Every essential conclusion has at least three routes, counted with the
   optional branches turned off.
2. **Every herring is resolved** by the true solution, and each one sits on a *real* secret, so the
   nerves it produces are honest.
3. **No Act I proof.** No proof category can be completed from cards available in Act I.
4. **Calibrated performance.** At least one honest witness who sounds nervous, and one calm liar.
5. **The killer appears early and helps you.**
6. **The IP check.** Compare the plot structurally with the famous works in the same subgenre. Generic
   devices are fine. Combinations that echo one specific plot are not.
7. **Sensitivity pass** on period portrayals and on any controversial content in a sponsored context.
8. **Run the adversarial review** (`references/adversarial-review.md`) before the publisher sees the bible.
