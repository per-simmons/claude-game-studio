# NARRATIVE: "Dead Air on the Meridian"

Case 1 of an original series. It's a closed-circle murder aboard a 1936 American sleeper train that's
stopped for one night in a mountain pass. Every character, name and plot here is original. The genre
devices it uses (a closed circle, a broken alibi, false identities, a helpful assistant) are generic
conventions, not anyone's protected expression (17 U.S.C. §102(b); RESEARCH.md §4.5). §12 lists the
deliberate distancing from specific copyrighted novels.

## 0. Logline and the two-layer twist

**Logline.** Desmond Crane is the most feared gossip voice in American radio. He signs off with *"This is
Desmond Crane, and I hear everything."* He's found beaten to death in his drawing room on the Meridian
Limited, which is stopped by a snow slide at Kestrel Pass. A rotary plow will reach it at dawn with the
county sheriff aboard. Everyone on Crane's car had a reason to fear him. You have four hours and their
voices.

**The twist:**
1. **Crane wasn't the blackmailer.** Two passengers have been paying blackmail in his name, and he knew
   nothing about it. His devoted secretary, Nora Pell, has run the racket for two years out of his
   research files. Tonight one of her victims came to Crane's door to pay in person, and he worked it out.
2. **The perfect alibi comes from an honest man with the wrong time.** Nora's alibi witness is a kind,
   truthful old colonel. Since his wife died he has kept his watch on Denver time, an hour ahead of the
   railroad's Pacific time. Everything he says is true, and every time he gives is an hour off.

**Why the case is built for voice.** The suspect who sounds guiltiest, the actress, is innocent. The
witness who sounds most honest is honest and still wrong. The killer is the calmest voice on the train.
Nerves lead you to secrets, and only evidence tells you which secret is murder.

## 1. Setting

- **The train:** *The Meridian Limited*, Train No. 7 westbound from Chicago to Oakland Pier on the
  fictional Great Meridian Railway. The fittings are Art Deco: chrome, burl walnut, frosted glass.
- **The night:** Saturday, December 19, 1936, running into Sunday the 20th. It's the **third night out**
  (the train left Chicago at 9:30 pm CT on Thursday the 17th). It's due at Oakland Pier on Sunday morning.
  Crane plans to broadcast his Sunday-night program live from a San Francisco station.
- **The stop:** At **11:05 p.m. Pacific** a snow slide covers the line at milepost 212, below Kestrel Pass,
  and the brakes go into emergency. Steam heat and lights stay on. A rotary plow out of Alder Flats will
  clear the line **at dawn, about 6:00**, and Sheriff Dunmore is riding with it. Once the train moves, the
  passengers scatter at Alder Flats.
- **Weather:** light snow that stops by 1:00. Prints and objects in the snow stay readable by lantern,
  half-covered.
- **The clock** (the hinge of the case): At **Cinder Junction**, a fictional division point, at about
  6:15 pm Pacific, the railroad changed from Mountain to Pacific time. The conductor announced *"Set
  your watches back one hour,"* and the dining-car menu card says so in print. **Every time in this
  document is Pacific (railroad) time unless it's marked otherwise.**
- **The crew's night:**
  - Conductor Briggs goes forward with the engineer and head brakeman to look at the slide at 11:08 and
    comes back at 1:30. The engine log records both.
  - The **flagman** goes out the back of *Sundown* at 11:06 with fusees. Railroad rule keeps him about a
    mile behind the train to protect it. He's unreachable until he's relieved at 4:30 (§7, proactive clue).

## 2. The cast

There are eight principals: the victim, six suspects and your partner. There's also one hidden witness.
Voice profiles are in VO-DIRECTION.md and visual specs in ART-BIBLE.md.

### The victim: Desmond Crane, 52
- He runs the Sunday-night program *Crane's Corner* and a syndicated column. He's vain, cruel and
  meticulous.
- He writes every item **longhand** in a black notebook and has **never typed a word in his life**. He
  dictates to Nora and calls typing "women's work."
- He carries a heavy chrome ribbon microphone (about 8 lb) in a fitted case for the remote broadcast,
  and declaims into it unplugged out of habit, at all hours.
- At dinner he ran his pencil down the manifest and announced: **"Sunday's broadcast will end a career on
  this very train."** He meant Dr. Ashcombe, a real scoop from his own files. Everyone at dinner thought
  he meant them.
- He also told Briggs to "wake me the moment there's word on the line. I have a broadcast."

### The killer: Nora Pell, 29, Crane's secretary (DR4)
- **Public face.** Four years with Crane. Quiet and precise, faintly warm, holding her grief in tight.
  She hands you Crane's black notebook and, with obvious regret, points out every passenger with a reason
  to hate him. Crane's book has an entry on her too: *"PELL — the Reno business ('33). Keep her
  grateful."* That's a quiet Reno divorce she hid from her family, a real and innocent secret. It makes
  her look like one more of his victims.
- **The truth.**
  - For two years she has copied items out of Crane's files and mailed typed letters to his targets,
    signed *"A Friend of Mr. Crane's."* Payment goes to **Post Office Box 1129, Chicago.** The victims all
    assume Crane is squeezing them.
  - She types the letters on her own portable typewriter. Its lowercase **e** has a cracked face that
    prints with a gap at the top. She types Crane's fair-copy scripts on the same machine.
- **Why tonight.**
  - At 11:45 Celeste Varga went to Crane's door to pay him in person. Crane had no idea what she meant.
    He read the letter, recognised his own files and Nora's broken *e*, and understood.
  - At 12:20 he knocked on Nora's door, *"In here. Now,"* and told her he'd wire the police from Alder
    Flats and have her detained at Oakland. He'd rather break the scandal himself, as its victim, than
    read about it in a rival's column.
  - He bluffed: *"Miss Varga has the letter, and she'll hand it to the police."* (In fact it's in his
    jacket.) Nora picked up the microphone.
  - Because of the bluff she never looked for the letter, and she steers you toward Celeste.
- **The alibi was an accident she turned into a weapon.**
  - She had taken her wristwatch off to type before dictation and left it on her typewriter in DR4.
  - In *Sundown* at 11:20 she asked the Colonel the time. He said "twenty past twelve," and she said
    "Heavens, so late."
  - At dinner she'd heard him boast that he never changes his watch.
  - After the murder she put the two together and built her story on his clock.
- **A line she gives freely.** Asked about E-DRAFTS or her job: *"He never touched a typewriter in his
  life, Agent. He called it women's work."* It's true. It traps her later (CL-NOR-4).
- **Why she's dangerous to play against.** She almost never shows a tell, she helps you, and she steers
  you toward real secrets that aren't murder.

### Celeste Varga, 38, film star (DR2)
- **Public face.** The screen's Hungarian-born "countess," on her way to San Francisco to shoot her
  comeback picture on location. Theatrical, gracious, imperious.
- **Secret.**
  - She is **Ruth Kowalczyk of Braddock, Pennsylvania.** The studio invented the countess.
  - At 17 she married a mill hand and **never divorced him**, so her marriage to a studio producer is
    bigamous. Exposure would mean the morals clause, possible prosecution, and the end of her career.
- **Blackmail.** She has paid $300 a month to Box 1129 for eight months. The December 10 letter demands
  **$2,000 "before Christmas."**
- **Tonight.**
  - She panicked at Crane's boast.
  - **11:45:** she went to DR3 with $2,000 in cash, her travelling coat over her nightdress. Crane said:
    *"Madam, I have never written you a letter in my life."* He read hers, went white, kept it, and said
    *"Someone has been using my name."*
  - **11:55:** she left with her money. Her **diamond ear-clip** fell under his seat.
  - **12:00:** her lover Teddy slipped into DR2, by arrangement.
  - **12:25:** through the connecting door they heard a man's raised voice, then someone answering *low and
    level* (she can't tell you who), then a heavy thump. Her travel clock read 12:25. Neither of them
    went out. Crane declaimed at all hours, and if they were found together in DR2 at midnight it would
    end them both.
- **Her lie.** "I went to bed at eleven and never left my compartment. I was alone."
- **Her tell.** Under pressure the Hungarian accent slips into flat Pittsburgh vowels.

### Teddy Lowell, 26, "of the Boston Lowells" (Bellflower, upper 9)
- **Public face.** A charming, idle heir. He and Celeste pretend not to know each other.
- **Secret.**
  - He is **Edward Lowell, a dance-hall instructor from Newark**, Celeste's lover and kept man. Same
    name, different class.
  - Crane's book: *"VARGA — & the dancing boy (Lowell, Newark)."*
- **Tonight.**
  - In the club car until last call. The steward was away in the diner's galley from 11:10 to 11:50
    making coffee for the crew, so Teddy poured his own.
  - He saw Nora go **aft at about eleven** and come **forward at about twenty to twelve**. He'll add "I'd
    had a few, don't hold me to it."
  - Left at **11:58** and went into DR2 at **12:00**. Heard the voices and the thump at 12:25. Left DR2
    for his berth at 12:45.
- **His lie.** "I played cards in the club car till one, with a salesman, Morton or Norton, from your
  Bellflower car."
  - The club car closed at midnight.
  - The steward says Teddy was the last one out, at 11:58.
  - There's no Morton or Norton on the manifest.
- **What he gives you freely.** Nora's movements at eleven and twenty to twelve. They fit his own false
  story, they're true, and he's a proven liar, so players tend to throw them out.
- **His tell.** He jokes, speeds up, and laughs a beat too early.

### Dr. Julian Ashcombe, 56, society physician (DR5)
- **Public face.** A dry, exact Chicago physician, travelling to a private consultation in San
  Francisco. He examined the body.
- **Secret.**
  - In 1934 he operated **drunk** on Philip Vandermeer Jr., 22, and the boy died on the table. Ashcombe
    signed the certificate "heart failure."
  - He has paid **$500 a month to Box 1129 for fourteen months.**
  - Crane's dinner boast was about him, and Crane really meant to broadcast it on Sunday. So Ashcombe
    believes Crane took his money *and* sold him out anyway.
- **Tonight.** Lying awake in DR5 with his reading lamp on:
  - **12:10:** the porter brings ice next door but one, and Crane snaps at him.
  - **~12:20:** a knock along the corridor, then Crane's voice raised briefly. He took it for Crane
    rehearsing into that microphone.
  - **12:30 by his watch:** water running in DR4 for several minutes, and the toilet flushing. "I
    assumed Miss Pell was washing out stockings."
  - **~12:33:** a cold draught along the floor, as if a window had been raised nearby.
- **His lies:**
  - **CL-ASH-1:** "I never had any dealings with Crane."
  - **CL-ASH-2:** "I took a sleeping draught at eleven and heard nothing until the porter knocked."
- **What he says honestly from the start** (he's a precise man):
  - Time of death: "Medically, between midnight and one. The porter tells me he brought ice at ten past, which narrows it."
  - One blow to the left temple. The wound has a **fine crosshatch, "like a sieve, or a grille."**
- **His tell.** Clinical language that gets more precise as he lies, a dry throat, *[clears throat]*.
- **Herrings attached to him:**
  - Blood on his right cuff, from the examination. He explains it at once and truthfully.
  - His cotton wool. He cleaned the wound with it (see E-FIBRE).
  - The strongest *real* motive of anyone except the killer.

### Colonel Ambrose Hargreave, 71, retired Army engineer (DR6)
- **Public face.** Bluff, booming and kind. Full of railroad-building stories. On his way to his
  daughter's in Sacramento for Christmas.
- **Secret.**
  - He was **Sergeant Hargreave**, Corps of Engineers, and promoted himself in 1919. His daughter married
    into Sacramento society on "the Colonel."
  - Crane's book: *"HARGREAVE — 'Colonel'?? War Dept. reply pending."*
  - At dinner Crane needled him: "Which regiment was that, Colonel?"
- **The watch.**
  - His wife Martha died in Denver in 1931 and is buried there. Since then he has kept his pocket watch
    on **Denver (Mountain) time.**
  - At dinner he only said, gruffly, *"I don't fiddle my watch for any railroad, sir."* The word "Denver"
    and Martha come out only when the watch is finally shown, and that is the payoff.
- **Tonight, true Pacific time:**
  - In *Sundown* with brandy from **10:40**.
  - Nora joins him at **10:58**.
  - **11:05:** the train stops, the brandy spills, Nora stumbles and he catches her arm.
  - **11:06:** the flagman passes through and out the back.
  - **11:20:** Nora asks the time.
  - **11:40:** Nora leaves.
  - **12:01:** he walks through the club car as the steward pulls down the bar shutter, and reaches DR6 at
    **12:02**.
- **What he tells you, all of it sincere, in his watch's times:**
  - "Miss Pell came in just before midnight and sat with me till twenty to one."
  - "We stopped at five past twelve. She nearly went over. I caught her."
  - "The steward was pulling down his shutter as I came through. One o'clock. I turned in." He offers
    this only once the *Box 1129* topic is open, when you ask how his night ended.
- **His lie** (**CL-HAR-1**) is only about his rank. On the alibi he's completely honest, and he takes
  offence if you press him on it, which sounds like guilt.
- **The watch gate.** Before you have a reason to ask, he won't show it: *"Martha gave me that watch,
  son. It isn't a party piece."* See §7.
- **His tell.** Louder and more parade-ground when he lies. Warm and rambling when he's honest.

### Ellis Monroe, 44, porter of *Larkspur*
- **Public face.** Eighteen years on the Meridian. Formal, observant, and discreet about passengers as a
  point of professional honour.
  - A card at the end of the car reads **YOUR PORTER: ELLIS MONROE.** Crane calls him "George" anyway.
    Ellis declines, very quietly, to answer to it.
  - The game treats him with full dignity. He's the sharpest witness on the train, and Briggs respects
    him.
- **Secret.**
  - Since Ogden he has hidden his **sister's girl, Dottie Hale, 16**, in the baggage car. Her family
    lost the Oklahoma farm, and she's running to her aunt in Fresno.
  - If the company finds out, he loses eighteen years and his pension.
- **Tonight:**
  - **11:15:** brings Crane water.
  - **11:25–11:50:** forward. He saw Dottie climb down onto the snow, on the side away from the crew, to
    look at the slide, and he went to hustle her back aboard. That's why nobody was on watch when Nora
    came forward at 11:40 or when Celeste knocked at 11:45.
  - **11:55:** back at his seat. Sees Miss Varga in the corridor, coat over her nightdress, coming from
    the direction of DR3.
  - **12:00:** Mr. Lowell slips into DR2.
  - **12:02:** the Colonel comes in from the club car to DR6.
  - **12:05:** DR3 rings. **12:10:** he delivers ice and soda. Crane is shaking, with pencil writing on a
    telegraph pad. Ellis: *"Any wires, sir? I can hold them for Alder Flats."* Crane: *"Not yet. Don't
    disturb me again tonight."*
  - **12:15–12:35:** in the baggage car with Dottie, with a blanket and sandwiches.
  - **12:35:** coming back, he hears a window bang shut somewhere mid-car and feels cold along the floor.
  - **12:45:** sees Mr. Lowell go forward.
  - **12:40 onward:** in the smoking room with the door half shut, worrying. The shoes sit unshined.
- **His lie** (**CL-ELL-1**): "From a quarter past twelve I was in the smoking room shining the shoes,
  sir."
  - Breaks on Briggs: *"Those shoes were sitting there chalked and dull when I came through at 1:35. I
    thought Ellis was behind tonight."*
  - Also breaks on E-PRINTS → the baggage car.
- **Trust tiers.**
  - **Tier 1, given freely:**
    - the ice at 12:10 and the "not yet" about wires;
    - "Two towels every room, every night";
    - "Mr. Crane never typed, sir. Called it women's work";
    - "Doctor's reading lamp was on under his door all night."
  - **Tier 2, only once his secret is out and you've treated him fairly (§7):**
    - the 11:55, 12:00, 12:02 and 12:45 sightings;
    - the window at 12:35;
    - **"While I was at my seat, Miss Pell never went aft, and nobody came forward from the club car at
      twenty to one."**
- **His tell.** He slips into company-rulebook language, precise and procedural, and over-explains
  railroad detail.

### Conductor Walter Briggs, 60: your partner (not a suspect)
- **Role.**
  - He's the Watson. Gravel-voiced, Midwestern, by the book.
  - **His railroad-standard watch is the game's true clock**, checked against the engine log.
  - He holds the log, the manifest and the steward's statement.
  - He does what you ask: fetch someone, search a room (with consent or cause), take a lantern into the
    snow, set up a face-off.
- **Alibi.** Out at the slide with the engine crew from 11:08 to 1:30.
- **Fair-play duty.** He reports everything he sees and never hides a thought (Knox 8 and 9).
- **Briggs's Gate.** He stands at the accusation: *"Once I wire the sheriff, there's no taking it back."*

### Hidden witness: Dottie Hale, 16
- Unlocked through Ellis.
- She confirms Ellis was with her from 12:15 to 12:35, and explains the **small bootprints** from 11:30.
- She carries the "report the stowaway?" decision. She knows nothing about the murder, and that's how it
  should be.

### Offstage: Steward Fisk, the club car steward
His statement reaches you through Briggs:
- "Galley from ten past eleven to ten to twelve, making crew coffee."
- "Saw Miss Pell go aft about eleven."
- "Mr. Lowell was the last one out, at two minutes to midnight."
- "Shutter down at midnight; the Colonel came through just as I pulled it."

## 4. The true solution (accusation key)

> **Nora Pell** killed Desmond Crane at about **12:25** by striking him with **his microphone**. He
> had found out that **she was blackmailing his targets in his name**, and he meant to have her detained
> at Oakland. Her alibi rests on Colonel Hargreave, who is honest and keeps Denver time. She was actually
> with him from 10:58 to 11:40.

**Accusation form** (GDD §8):
- **WHO, HOW, WHEN, WHY**, each picked from a word bank built from what the player has discovered.
- **PROOF**: exactly three cards. **Only contradiction cards (C-\*) count**, meaning pairs the player has
  linked on the board. Raw testimony and raw evidence don't count. The three must cover all three
  categories:

| Category | Valid cards |
|---|---|
| **Alibi** | **C-DENVER** (E-WATCH or T-DENVER + CL-NOR-1) · **C-SHUTTER** (the Colonel's "shutter at one o'clock" + E-CLUBRULE/steward "midnight"; it breaks CL-NOR-1) · **C-BEDTIME** (Ellis T2 "Colonel to bed 12:02" or "nobody came forward at 12:40" + CL-NOR-1) |
| **Motive** | **C-TELEGRAM** (E-PAD + E-SHREDS: "BY ONE IN MY EMPL—") · **C-TYPE** (CL-NOR-4 + Nora's "he never touched a typewriter", or Ellis T1 "never typed") · **C-BROKEN-E** (E-LETTER + E-FAIRCOPY or E-TYPEWRITER) |
| **Physical** | **C-TOWEL** (E-TOWEL + E-TOWELCOUNT) · **C-FIBRE** (E-FIBRE + E-TOWEL, after Ashcombe clears his wool) · **C-WATER** (T-WATER + CL-NOR-1 "in *Sundown* till twenty to one") |

**C-STOP** (Nora's "we stopped just after I sat down, around midnight" + E-LOG 11:05) is a **stepping
stone**, not proof. It proves she was in *Sundown* at 11:05. It opens the Colonel's watch gate and takes
a chunk of Nora's composure. Her answer: *"Then I'm wrong about when I went aft, Agent. I'd no watch.
The Colonel will tell you I stayed till twenty to one."*

## 7. Branching map

The structure is a **node-based investigation** inside a three-act **branch-and-bottleneck**. Every
essential conclusion has at least three clues.

```
ACT I — "The Room"  (1:55 → ~3:00 game time; open nodes, any order)
  Briggs briefing ─► DR3 scene (E-MIC, E-FIBRE, E-BUCKET, E-PAD, E-LETTER, E-DRAFTS, E-EARRING)
     ├─► Ashcombe exam (E-WOUND, TOD) ── fibre misdirect → "the doctor's wool?"
     ├─► Nora hands over E-BOOK ──► every secret looks like motive (including her own "Reno")
     └─► Celeste (E-EARRING) · Teddy (sightings, false alibi) · Colonel (alibi for Nora) · Ellis (T1)

BOTTLENECK 1 — "He never wrote to me"   (T-NEVERWROTE / T-BROKEN-E / E-DRAFTS reasoning)
  Routes: crack Celeste via E-EARRING · via Ellis T2 · crack Ashcombe via E-BOOK + Box 1129 ·
          E-LETTER (typed) vs E-DRAFTS (longhand) + Ellis T1 "never typed"
  ► Reframe: Crane wasn't the blackmailer. Who had his files and a typewriter?

ACT II — "The Machine"  (~3:00 → ~4:30)
  Nora composure hit ─► DR4 search (E-TYPEWRITER, E-FAIRCOPY, E-TOWELCOUNT, E-WRISTWATCH) ─► C-TYPE / C-BROKEN-E
  Snow search ─► E-TOWEL, E-SHREDS (→ C-TELEGRAM), E-PRINTS (herring → Dottie)
  Ashcombe cracks (CL-ASH-2) ─► T-WATER; he clears his wool ─► C-FIBRE
  Celeste + Teddy face-off ─► T-THUMP
  Ellis branch ─► Dottie ─► tier 2

BOTTLENECK 2 — "The Denver Time"   (T-DENVER)
  Watch gate opens on any of: E-MENU read (after Bottleneck 1) · C-STOP · the Colonel's "shutter at one" vs E-CLUBRULE ·
                              Ellis T2 "Colonel to bed at 12:02" · the flagman at 4:30
  ► Nora's alibi falls apart.

ACT III — "Dead Air"  (whenever the player chooses)
  Optional face-off: Nora + the Colonel ("What time do you make it, Colonel?")
  Briggs's Gate ─► ACCUSATION ─► endings
```

out the back. Right after we stopped. Eleven-oh-six by my watch."* His line stands in for E-LOG in C-STOP
(flagman + CL-NOR-2), and the watch gate opens.

## 9. Fair-play audit

- **Knox 1 / Van Dine 10.** The culprit is the first suspect you meet. She hands you the book.
- **Knox 2–4 / Van Dine 8, 14.** Nothing supernatural, no secret passages, no unknown poisons. The
  method is a microphone.
- **Knox 6.** No accident or intuition solves it. Every crack is an evidence event. The inner voice
  comments on performance, never on truth. The watch gate means the case can't be solved by luck by
  asking the time.
- **Knox 8–9.** Briggs reports everything.
- **Knox 10.** No twins. The false identities belong to innocents and are proved by evidence.
- **Van Dine 11, 12, 17, 19.** One culprit with a personal motive, not a professional criminal.
- **Van Dine 1, 15.** Every clue is shown plainly. On a rewatch the viewer hears the Colonel say "I don't
  fiddle my watch" at dinner, Nora say "he never touched a typewriter," and Teddy give the real times.

## 12. IP distancing

The reviewer flagged that Rev 1 stacked several generic elements that, together, echo *Murder on the
Orient Express* (1934, under US copyright until 2030). Rev 2 removes the specific echoes:

- **No bolt business.** The DR2/DR3 connecting door is a neutral herring, and no one is asked to check
  a bolt.
- **No burnt-paper fragment read from ashes.** The telegram is **flushed onto the snow**, an original,
  period-specific clue tied to the "refrain from flushing" plate.
- **No robe or kimono.** Celeste wears a travelling coat over her nightdress.
- **No stopped watch on the body.**
- **The train is stopped for hours by a slide and freed by a plow at dawn,** not snowbound for days.
- **One killer.** Not a group, not a jury, and the motive isn't revenge for an old crime.
- **The secretary angle differs.** The killer is the victim's own secretary, which differs from *The
  Mystery of the Blue Train*. This is low risk.
- **Not the *Roger Ackroyd* trick.** The letter that exposes the racket is a victim's **money demand**,
  not a confession that names the blackmailer. Crane receives no letter that names Nora.
- **Ellery Queen's phrase is never used on screen.** The gate is "Briggs's Gate."
- **No borrowed names.** "Meridian Limited," "Great Meridian Railway," "Kestrel Pass," "Cinder Junction"
  and "Alder Flats" are invented. Before any on-screen map, check that none of them collides with a real
  railroad trademark or a real place.
